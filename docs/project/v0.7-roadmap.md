# OpenGGF 0.7 Roadmap

Date: 2026-08-25

Branch: `next`

Status: active roadmap for the 0.7 candidate

OpenGGF 0.7 is the complete Sonic 3 & Knuckles campaign release. Its primary
product goal is to make the locked-on game playable from Angel Island through
each character's ROM-authored finale, with the corresponding ending sequence.
The release also graduates the substantial modding, Time Attack, multiplayer,
editor, compatibility, and S3K work already staged on `next`, with Time Attack
and multiplayer leaving the engine artifact to ship as a bundled mod.

This is a release roadmap, not a claim that every item named below is complete.
The status labels distinguish work already present on `next` from work that must
still satisfy its release gate.

## Release outcomes

0.7 is ready when all of the following are true:

1. Knuckles can complete his locked-on route from AIZ through the Sky Sanctuary
   finale and reach his complete outro.
2. Tails can complete his locked-on route from AIZ through Death Egg and reach
   his complete outro.
3. Sonic can complete his locked-on route from AIZ through Death Egg, enter
   Doomsday when the ROM emerald condition selects it, and reach the matching
   complete outro.
4. A manifest-backed complete-chain trace for each required route outcome
   drives one continuous engine through the full route, its represented stage
   detours, finale, outro, and terminal movie tail with no unexpected
   comparison error.
5. The three routes work without debug warps, trace-state hydration, manually
   fitted fixture constants, or game/zone carve-outs in shared runtime code.
6. Game-wide behavioral differences have a typed policy home beneath
   `GameModule`, without turning data, art, zone behavior, or runtime factories
   into a universal policy bag.
7. Existing `next` features retained for 0.7 pass their stated release gates and
   are documented honestly; unfinished optional polish remains explicitly
   deferred.

## Scope of a complete character route

The route gate follows the shipped locked-on game's character-specific
progression. A level is not complete merely because it loads or can be crossed
with debug movement.

| Character | Sonic 3 half | Sonic & Knuckles half | Finale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knuckles | AIZ, HCZ, MGZ, CNZ, ICZ, LBZ | MHZ, FBZ, SOZ, LRZ, HPZ, SSZ | Knuckles' Sky Sanctuary/Mecha Sonic conclusion and outro; DEZ/DDZ are not part of his ROM route |
| Tails | AIZ, HCZ, MGZ, CNZ, ICZ, LBZ | MHZ, FBZ, SOZ, LRZ, HPZ, SSZ, DEZ | Tails' Death Egg conclusion and outro; DDZ is not part of his ROM route |
| Sonic | AIZ, HCZ, MGZ, CNZ, ICZ, LBZ | MHZ, FBZ, SOZ, LRZ, HPZ, SSZ, DEZ | Normal Death Egg conclusion, or DDZ and the good ending when the emerald condition selects it |

For every listed zone, completion includes:

- the correct character layout, start position, object table, collision paths,
  foreground/background deformation, animated art, palette behavior, water,
  music, and level events;
- traversal-critical objects, hazards, badniks, minibosses, bosses, capsules,
  signposts, and character-specific route gates;
- act-to-act, zone-to-zone, special-stage, bonus-stage, death, restart, and
  save/resume transitions used by the route;
- sidekick behavior where the selected team has a sidekick, without allowing
  sidekick defects to block or corrupt the main-character route;
- rewind-safe object and event ownership for gameplay state reachable during
  the route; and
- native 320x224 behavior plus the supported viewport/team compatibility
  contracts already advertised by `next`.

## Milestone 1 — Lock the game-policy architecture before Mod API publication

**Status:** planned; architectural direction approved

`GameModule` remains the composition and lifecycle root. The change introduces
a typed policy subtree rather than a superclass or a second competing root.

Target boundaries:

- Add an immutable `GamePolicies` aggregate for game-wide behavioral facts.
- Move `GameRules` ownership out of `PhysicsProvider`; camera, rings, objects,
  sidekicks, power-ups, and drowning rules are not children of physics.
- Keep narrow records such as `CollisionRules`, `CameraRules`, and `RingRules`
  as the consumers' dependencies rather than passing the entire aggregate.
- Separate audio behavioral rules from ROM-backed asset loading and stateful
  sound-driver construction. `GameAudioProfile.createSmpsLoader(Rom)` is a
  factory concern, even though the current profile also contains policy.
- Keep art, mappings, PLC/DPLC data, ROM offsets, registries, zone events,
  object-family behavior, and mutable session services with their existing
  smallest accurate providers or owners.
- Keep factories and lifecycle callbacks on module-owned runtime components;
  policies must be immutable and must not acquire session state.
- Support cross-game donation through explicit capability-specific composers.
  Do not add a generic mechanism that can arbitrarily replace any policy
  category.
- Preserve the rule-placement admission checklist and add guards against a new
  broad feature bag or game-name branches in shared runtime code.

This milestone must land before the first Mod API 0.7 baseline is published.
The candidate currently exposes gameplay policy types, so the migration must
update the recursive API inventory, creator documentation, sample mods, and
compatibility evidence together. Once a baseline is published, policy reshaping
must follow the published compatibility contract rather than silently rewriting
it.

Gate: all three stock modules expose the same typed policy shape; shared
consumers depend on their narrow category; supported donation combinations are
explicitly tested; invalid generic mixtures are not representable through a
public composer; and module/bootstrap, rewind, audio, trace, and Mod API guards
remain green.

## Planned architecture work — Extract Time Attack and multiplayer into a bundled mod

**Status:** planned for 0.7 on `next`; sequenced after Milestone 0 and Milestone 1
and before the Mod API baseline is published. Added 2026-09-08.

Time Attack and multiplayer ghost racing add no ROM behavior. They are the
largest non-accuracy surface on `next` and the only one that lives inside the
engine rather than beside it. The base engine should carry ROM accuracy and the
mod framework; addon gameplay features belong in mods that consume that
framework. This work moves the feature out of the engine jar into a bundled,
first-party mod and treats it as the first real consumer of the Mod API.

### Measured surface at `next` 949e49072

- `game/timeattack/` (32 files), `net/` (56 files, ~8.3k lines),
  `game/ghost/` (14 files), `tools/net/` and `tools/verifier/`; 82 test files.
- Engine coupling: ~183 lines in `GameLoop`, ~190 in `Engine`, ~85 in
  `MasterTitleScreen`, ~35 in `LevelRenderer`, plus `GameServices`,
  `GameStateManager`, and `GameplayModeContext` references. `GameLoop` and
  `Engine.draw` are already size-ratcheted by `-Pguards`.
- The feature's own design (`docs/architecture/designs/2026-07-04-multiplayer-time-attack-design.md`)
  is ghost-only with no physics synchronization, built on the shared
  `game/recording` stack that already exists on `develop`. The engine-facing
  contract is therefore small even though the feature is large.
- `GhostRenderRegistry` and `AttemptInputRecording` are already `@ModApi`
  roots; the remaining coupling is direct construction and frame wiring.

### Why before publication

`ModContext` currently registers game modules, patches, objects, characters,
art, zones, launch teams, input filters, and HUD profiles. It has no master-title
entry contribution, no frame-lifecycle hook, no overlay/HUD render contribution,
and no recording-consumer hook. Hosting Time Attack requires those seams, and
adding them changes the recursive signature inventory. Doing this after the
baseline is published would turn an ordinary refactor into a compatibility
decision under the release-line policy. If the framework cannot host the
project's own largest feature, it is not ready to publish.

### Target shape

- Engine keeps: `game/recording`, deterministic input recording and replay,
  `GhostRenderRegistry` as a generic ghost-sprite render seam, and the
  `ExternalContentPolicy` gate that keeps network and external content out of
  certifying trace/test sessions.
- Mod owns: track catalog, attempt state machine, split/delta HUD, `.ggfghost`
  store, identity, all of `net/`, room/vote/standings/spectate presentation,
  minimap and nameplates, and the Time Attack menu.
- Separate processes stay separate: master, relay, host, verifier, bot, and
  load-test tools move out of the engine artifact entirely.
- New Mod API seams, each generic rather than Time Attack shaped:
  1. master-title entry contribution (a mod-owned screen reachable from the
     master title, alongside the existing exclusive game-start destination);
  2. gameplay frame-lifecycle hook (level start, level end/finish, restart,
     pause) delivered after the engine's own frame step and never able to
     mutate gameplay state;
  3. overlay/HUD render contribution layered above the existing row-only HUD
     profile; and
  4. recording-consumer hook that receives finished deterministic recordings.
- Sample and Flappy mods remain outside the engine jar; verify the native S3K
  Tails retarget left no engine-side special cases.

### Constraints

- The extraction must not change native 320x224 timing, trace comparison, or
  any gameplay owner. Hooks observe; they do not drive.
- Rewind, save, and audio ownership stay with the engine; the mod uses the
  published playable-subclass rewind surface and tagged saves like any other
  mod.
- Certifying sessions run with the mod absent, not merely disabled, so the
  engine's own tests do not depend on it.
- Arbitrary non-ROM team definitions are a candidate for the same treatment:
  keep the launch-team capability in the engine and move the non-ROM team
  list into a bundled mod so a stock install offers only ROM teams. Decide
  this separately; it does not block the Time Attack extraction.

Gate: `game/timeattack/`, `net/`, `game/ghost/` racing code, and the
`tools/net` and `tools/verifier` entry points are absent from the engine
artifact; the bundled mod builds through `ggfmod`, passes its own suite, and
reproduces the Milestone 8 Time Attack gate; the four new seams are in the
signature inventory with creator documentation and at least one non-Time-Attack
sample using each; `-Pguards` size ratchets for `GameLoop` and `Engine.draw`
tighten rather than loosen; and the Mod API 0.7 baseline is published only
after this lands.

## Planned performance work — Asynchronous ROM loading

**Status:** planned for 0.7 on `next`; deferred from 0.6. Added 2026-09-05.

Reduce host frame hitches by preparing ROM-backed assets on bounded workers
while the game executes existing PLC/decompression waits, fades, and title-card
loading transitions. Start with standalone object/boss art; extend incremental
queues only after the simpler path demonstrates a measurable benefit.

### Existing timing foundation on `next`

Build on the existing simulated load-time and trace-timing infrastructure:
`HardwareTimingService`, `LoadTimeProfileFactory`, `ProfiledLoadTimeManifest`,
`RomWorkBudgetScheduler`, and recorded completion admission. This work already
separates CPU preparation from observable readiness. Preserve its configured
load-time profiles, native service budgets, and recorded completion boundaries
while moving eligible CPU preparation onto workers.

The existing timing owners continue to determine when completion is visible;
the new worker layer uses those waiting periods to prepare bytes. Host elapsed
time and worker completion order must not change simulated frame counts or
replace recorded lag/main-loop admission. Profile-based readiness applies only
to work submitted through the timing service; game-owned PLC and dynamic-art
lifecycles retain their own service rules. Audit each candidate's actual owner
before connecting it to workers, and preserve existing mode/fallback behavior.

This is an extension of the timing implementation already on `next`. At kickoff,
inventory its current game/kind support and fixture coverage separately; the
cross-game contract alone does not establish implemented or tested coverage.

### Ownership and timing constraints

- Separate host preparation from game-visible submission, service, completion,
  and rendering. A cache/prefetch operation must not invent a ROM queue job or
  make a future submission visible early.
- Workers consume immutable ROM data and produce independently owned CPU-side
  results. Keep queue mutation, gameplay changes, art registration, destination
  tile writes, renderer refresh, and GL uploads on their existing owning thread.
- An early worker result waits for the ROM-modeled service boundary. Preserve
  FIFO order, clear-versus-append behavior, incremental pattern/module delivery,
  destination overwrites, and all observable queue/decoder progress. A single
  `Future.isDone()` flag cannot replace the resumable queue model.
- If preparation misses its deadline, wait at the required host boundary rather
  than advance gameplay or insert extra emulated wait frames. This fallback can
  still hitch. Any responsive presentation during the wait needs an explicit
  input/audio/clock policy that preserves deterministic replay.
- Bound pending jobs, decoded bytes, and cache retention. Identify results by
  ROM/content identity and the owning session/transition generation; reject
  obsolete completions after cancellation, restart, ROM replacement, or rewind.
- Rewind captures deterministic modeled progress, not worker scheduling. Restores
  may reuse valid immutable results or re-prepare them without allowing the host
  completion order to change gameplay.
- Preserve the [hardware-timing authority contract](../architecture/designs/2026-07-27-cross-game-hardware-timing-trace-contract.md):
  recorded timing may admit only matching, prepared, production-submitted work.
  Worker timing never becomes trace authority or supplies gameplay values.

### Delivery sequence

1. Measure first-use art loads and existing loading waits: CPU preparation,
   queue service, GPU upload, allocation, retained bytes, and frame-time tails.
   Integrate relevant `develop` performance fixes before choosing the baseline.
   Include the existing load-time profiles and recorded admission in that
   baseline; identify reusable preparation boundaries before adding worker APIs.
2. Separate pure CPU preparation from publication for standalone object/boss
   sheets, then use a bounded worker during production loading transitions.
   Evaluate earlier cache preparation without changing ROM submission timing.
3. Prove early/late completion, cancellation, failures, restart, and rewind on
   this path before considering incremental Nemesis/PLC and Kosinski/KosM queues.
   Preserve their distinct service and partial-visibility contracts.
4. Extend only where measurements justify it, with per-game owners supplying
   semantic scheduling rules rather than shared game/zone-name branches.

Starting points to inspect are `Sonic3kPlcLoader.preDecompress`, the deferred
level-resource owners, and the preparation/readiness separation in the hardware
timing service. These are useful boundaries, not a claim that those loaders
already execute asynchronously; re-check the current implementation at kickoff.

Acceptance: compare synchronous and asynchronous results across representative
loading transitions and multiple recordings, in both supported live load-time
modes and recorded-timing replay, including deliberately reordered,
early, late, failed, and cancelled worker completions. Require identical asset
bytes, queue state, partial tile visibility, input/audio progression, and rewind
outcomes; no new trace divergences; bounded memory and shutdown; and measured
improvement in loading-frame latency or allocation. Run the relevant art/mapping,
queue, timing-authority, rewind, ordinary-suite, and fresh-JVM guard checks. Do
not publish an async-loading or hitch-free claim before this evidence exists.

## Planned audio work — Live FM core switching

**Status:** planned for 0.7 on `next`; designed during the 0.6 fast FM
delivery. Added 2026-09-06.

0.6 ships two FM cores behind `audio.fmCore`: `accurate` (the cycle-exact
Nuked-OPN2 port, about 1 ms per frame on the audio thread) and `fast` (the
clean-room register-level core, about 0.15 ms, the default; 178 of 178 oracle
vectors within tolerance). Changing the setting takes effect at the next
physical device, so comparing the two by ear means restarting. This item makes
the choice a live, shippable option with one core running in steady state.

### Design

The cores cannot share internal state: Nuked's is cycle-level, the fast core's
is frame-level, and translating between them would require reading the port's
synthesis state, which the fast core's clean-room rule forbids. They do accept
the same register stream, so a standby core is a cold object plus the live
register image.

- A `FmChip` wrapper owns the active core and records the live register image
  (both ports, 256 registers each, DAC enable and data, mutes, pan) through the
  existing chip write observer. Steady-state cost: none.
- On a switch request the wrapper resets the standby core, replays the image in
  canonical order (voices, levels, pitches, pans, LFO and timers, then key-ons)
  and runs both cores.
- During the warm-up the standby's sustained notes attack and decay to the live
  core's level. The wrapper correlates the two outputs over a short window and
  crossfades over about 20 ms once they agree, or after a 500 ms timeout; the
  old core is then dropped. Both cores run only for that fraction of a second.
- Rewind carries the active core's snapshot plus the core id, so a restore
  across a switch reinstates whichever core was live at that time.
- The setting stays `audio.fmCore`; a debug hotkey (off by default) flips it in
  play; the physical audio parity capture keeps refusing the fast core.

### What the listener hears

Notes sounding at the instant of the switch re-attack in the standby core,
hidden by the crossfade wait; drums and short SFX are unaffected because the
next one starts identically on both cores. Nothing after the switch. The
accurate core's audio-thread cost still applies once it is live, so the
setting must say that a machine only just keeping up may hear dropouts after
switching to `accurate`.

### Acceptance

- A scripted-DSP delegation test: every write, DAC call, mute and reset reaches
  the active core and the register image; the output follows the selection.
- A switch-during-sustain oracle test: switch in the middle of a held chord
  with LFO on and check the crossfaded output against a never-switched run of
  the destination core (waveform correlation above the corpus bound after the
  crossfade, no sample outside the crossfade window differing).
- A rewind round-trip across a switch.
- The fast-core corpus, the ordinary suite and the trace suite unchanged with
  the wrapper in place and `fast` selected.
- CONFIGURATION.md and the controls guide updated; the listening-test plan
  gains an A/B step that no longer needs a restart.

### Cheaper first step

If the full design slips, apply the switch at the next music start (level load,
act change, 1-up return) with no warm-up or crossfade: about two hours of work,
and the setting reads "takes effect at the next track".

  three outros;
- no regression relative to the recorded integration baseline, including no
  worsened pre-existing red;
- final `README`, configuration, creator/player guide, known-discrepancy,
  detailed release ledger, public release summary, and changelog updates; and
- Time Attack and multiplayer extracted into a bundled mod, with the engine
  artifact free of `game/timeattack/`, `net/`, and the network/verifier tools;
  and
- publication of the Mod API 0.7 baseline only after the candidate surface and
  migration guidance are final.

## Explicit non-goals

The following do not block 0.7 unless later evidence shows that they block one
of the outcomes above:

- arbitrary mixing of every policy category between games;
- replacing `GameModule` with an inheritance hierarchy;
- broad S1/S2 architecture migration unrelated to a release regression;
- every optional mod/editor item preserved in the deferred backlog;
- unsupported character/zone combinations not present in the shipped locked-on
  routes; and
- trace-specific gameplay corrections, fixture-fitted constants, or runtime
  asset fallbacks to the disassembly tree.
